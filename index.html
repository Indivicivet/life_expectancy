Method & sources

The baseline and four factors come from EPIC-Heidelberg, a German cohort of 22,469 adults. Activity, alcohol and sleep use mortality estimates from later meta-analyses, converted to life years using the same reconstructed mortality curve. Combining these studies assumes independent effects; the combined estimates have not been validated. Presets are examples, not population averages.

A millimort is a 1-in-1,000 chance of death; the 10-year figures use the reconstructed EPIC mortality curve and the selected mortality multipliers. A microlife is 30 minutes of expected life, spread across the comparison group's remaining years.

These are observational associations, not causal predictions or medical advice. Alcohol uses the published mortality estimates, even when their confidence intervals include no effect. Low-dose estimates do not establish benefit or safety. Long sleep and low BMI may reflect underlying illness.

RR is relative risk; HR is hazard ratio. The lifestyle editor shows original study estimates and 95% confidence intervals where reported. Chart whiskers show how each comparison changes at its source interval endpoints with all other effects and the baseline held fixed. Interpolated activity levels have no published interval. The whiskers cannot be added to obtain a confidence interval for the total.
