Method & sources

The baseline and four factors come from EPIC-Heidelberg, a German cohort of 22,469 adults. All seven factors modify the same reconstructed mortality curve, integrated from the user's current age to 110. The curve is calibrated at age 40 and extrapolation outside the cohort's observed ages is especially uncertain. Combining studies assumes independent effects; the combined estimates have not been validated. Presets are examples, not population averages.

A millimort is a 1-in-1,000 chance of death; the 10-year figures use the reconstructed EPIC mortality curve and the selected mortality multipliers. A microlife is 30 minutes of expected life, spread across the comparison group's remaining years.

These are observational associations, not causal predictions or medical advice. Alcohol uses the continuous, pooled 2006 fit for studies excluding former and light drinkers from the abstainer reference. Newer 2023 sex-specific estimates are shown separately and suggest substantially less low-dose protection. Neither establishes causal benefit or safety. Long sleep and low BMI may reflect underlying illness.

RR is relative risk; HR is hazard ratio. The lifestyle editor shows study estimates and source 95% confidence intervals where reported. Alcohol uses an estimated coefficient-limit sensitivity range because coefficient covariance was not published. Chart whiskers vary the source limits with all other effects and the baseline held fixed. Interpolated activity levels have no published interval. Whiskers cannot be added to obtain a confidence interval for the total.
